# Prompt for Claude Design

Copy everything below the line into Claude Design. Replace `[AppName]` first if you
have a name by then.

---

Design a three-page marketing website for **[AppName]**, a blind texting dating app.
You start with words only — no photo, no bio, no grid to swipe. As a conversation
genuinely deepens, pieces of the other person's profile appear. You earn the picture
by earning the conversation.

## Audience

People who tried dating apps, decided "this isn't for me," and left. People who
struggle to meet anyone in person and know it. People who want an introduction — a
nudge, the way a friend introduces you — so starting a conversation doesn't feel like
an audition. They are anxious, a little tired, and more interesting than they are
photogenic. Design should lower the stakes, never raise them.

## Voice

Warm and plainspoken. Calm, human, low-hype. No startup bravado, no exclamation
marks, no "revolutionizing." Short sentences. Speak to one nervous person, not a
market. Copy should sound like a friend explaining why they think you'd like this.

## Visual direction

- **Palette:** white-dominant. Purple as the accent and emphasis color; soft lavender
  tints for cards and section backgrounds. Keep large areas of white — the page
  should feel like it has room to breathe.
- **Shape language:** soft and rounded. Generous corner radii, gentle diffuse shadows,
  pill buttons. Chat bubbles are the recurring visual motif across all three pages.
- **Type:** a highly readable humanist sans. Comfortable sizes, generous line height,
  measure capped around 65 characters. Readability over cleverness.
- **Imagery:** no stock photos of couples. The product itself — message threads,
  blurred-to-sharp profile fragments — is the imagery.
- **Layout:** mobile-first. Design at ~390px width first, then scale up to tablet and
  desktop. Single-column, thumb-friendly tap targets, sticky bottom CTA on mobile.

## Primary CTA

**"Join the waitlist"** everywhere. Do not show a price anywhere on the site.

## Page 1 — Landing

**Hero**
- One-line explanation of the app.
- A supporting line for the skeptic who has deleted three dating apps already.
- Primary CTA: Join the waitlist. Email field + button, nothing else.
- Visual: two or three phone-framed message threads, slightly overlapped and tilted,
  showing a real-feeling conversation with the profile area still blank/greyed. One
  thread should show a fragment just beginning to resolve.

**Scroll sections, in order**

1. **Philosophy.** Why talk first. Part of why meeting someone is exciting is that
   they're new, fun, and a little mysterious — a profile deletes all three before a
   word is exchanged. This gives mystery back on purpose.
2. **How the reveal works.** A visual progression in four steps: text only → first
   fragments (a line of bio, a blurred sliver of a photo) → more of the profile
   resolves → full reveal. Make the blur-to-focus transition the centerpiece of this
   section. Emphasize: it's mutual. Neither person can unlock the other alone.
3. **How to get the most out of it.** Talking honestly means asking questions, not
   just answering them — actually trying to know someone rather than spamming
   messages to farm a reward. Doing that raises a private quality score, which earns
   more conversations per week, faster reveals, and pairing with other people who
   also show up honestly.
4. **Pricing.** Free trial, then a one-time payment. Not a subscription. Frame it
   honestly: we do not want you here forever, and leaving because you met someone is
   the intended ending. No number — say the price will be reasonable and is coming.
5. **Closing CTA.** Repeat the waitlist signup.

## Page 2 — What's wrong with dating apps

A market-research page. Make the data legible and calm — informative, not doom-scroll.
Purple-accented charts and stat cards on white, no red alarm styling.

**Use these figures (all real):**
- Match Group active users down **37%** year-over-year, March 2026
- Tinder revenue **−5.2%** in 2025 — its first annual decline ever
- Bumble revenue **−9.5%** in 2025
- Tinder paying users fell 9.6M (2024) → **8.8M** (Q4 2025)
- Average active users per app now **12–15M**, down from 50–70M in 2019–20
- **78%** of users report dating app burnout
- **88%** of men and **90%** of women say they're often or sometimes disappointed by
  people they meet through apps
- **48%** of online daters have experienced at least one unwanted behavior
**Then give the Hinge exception its own section — it is the most persuasive part of
the page, not a footnote.** Headline idea: "One app is growing. It's worth asking why."

- **Hinge grew revenue 25%**, paying users up 31%, while everything else fell.
- It did two things differently: it promised to be **"designed to be deleted,"** and
  it replaced the blank profile with **prompts** — specific questions that get a real
  answer out of someone.
- Both worked. Both are also half-measures, and say so plainly and without sneering:
  an app that bills you monthly is still paid to keep you, and prompts you read
  *before* you ever speak are still a profile you're judged on.

Then a small three-row comparison — Hinge / [AppName]:
1. *Designed to be deleted* — positioning on a subscription **vs.** a one-time
   payment, so the business is structurally finished with you.
2. *Prompts* — questions on a profile you read first **vs.** the conversation itself
   is the profile; there's nothing to read before you talk.
3. *Photos* — the first thing you see **vs.** earned, mutually, through real
   conversation.

Land it in one line: Hinge showed where the demand went. We don't think it went far
enough.

**Then four structural reasons, each paired with what [AppName] does instead.**
A two-column or stacked "the problem / our answer" pattern works well here:

1. *Appearance is the first and only filter* → we remove the photo until it's earned.
2. *Abundance kills investment* → seven conversations a week, no infinite queue.
3. *Mixed intent shares one interface* → the format itself filters; people looking
   for a quick swipe won't sit through a conversation.
4. *Subscriptions are paid to keep you single* → one-time payment, built to be left.
   Even "designed to be deleted" bills you monthly until you do.

Close with a short section: the number of people who want a real connection has not
dropped. The product they were handed did. Then the waitlist CTA.

## Page 3 — Features

Detailed but scannable. Cards, icons, and small product mockups. Sections:

1. **How conversations are found.** You get about **7 new blind conversations a week**.
   No browsing, no grid, no ranking by face. Scarcity is the feature — when you only
   get seven, you show up for them. Visualize the weekly allowance as something
   tangible (seven slots, a week view), not as a counter.

2. **The reveal system, in detail.** The most important section on the page. Show the
   full progression with real mockups: text-only thread → first bio line appearing →
   blurred photo sharpening in stages → full profile. Explain the rules plainly:
   - It's mutual. One person can't unlock the other.
   - It's driven by depth — two-sided conversation — not message count.
   - Volume alone does nothing. Spamming will not get you a photo.

3. **Chatting.** What the conversation view feels like. Calm, uncluttered, no read
   receipts anxiety, no pressure timers.

4. **Icebreakers.** Prompts for when you don't know how to start, and for when a
   conversation stalls. Show three or four real example prompts, written in the same
   warm voice — curious and specific, never cheesy. Frame these as the thing prompts
   were always supposed to be: a way into a conversation, not decoration on a profile
   someone reads before deciding whether you're worth talking to.

5. **Games.** Lightweight in-conversation games that get you talking rather than
   performing — two-truths variants, would-you-rather, guess-the-answer. Show a
   couple of examples in-context inside a thread.

6. **Activity ideas for meeting in person.** Once you're ready, real suggestions for
   low-pressure first meetings that aren't drinks at a bar. Show six or so as cards.

7. **Your quality score.** Explain it as a measure of how you talk, not how you look.
   Asking questions and engaging in good faith raises it. It unlocks more people per
   week, faster reveals, occasional early fragments, and pairing with other high-
   quality conversationalists. Keep the visualization private and gentle — a personal
   indicator, never a public rank or a number shown to other people.

Close with the waitlist CTA.

---

## Constraints

- Mobile-first. Every section must work in a single column at 390px.
- No price anywhere on the site.
- No stock photography of couples or people on phones.
- Keep body copy short. If a section needs three paragraphs, it needs a diagram.
- Every message mockup should contain plausible, specific, human dialogue — not
  "Hey 👋 / Hey!" filler. The mockups are the strongest selling tool on the site, so
  write them like real conversations worth having.
